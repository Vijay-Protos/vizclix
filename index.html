One Project, Start to Finish.

The two towers from the flight, scoped as a real launch. A worked example, not a past job: how we plan a tower launch, written out in full so you can see the thinking before you hire it. [ Replaced by a real case study once a VizClix shoot is delivered ]

/ 01 — The brief

A two-tower residential launch on a main road, hills behind, a lake to one side. Sales needed a film for the launch event and stills for the hoarding, four weeks out. The towers were topped out but unclad on one face.

/ 02 — The constraints
  • An unclad façade that could not appear in the hero frames
  • A monsoon week in the middle of the window
  • Neighbouring towers close on the approach side
  • Airspace to be read and cleared before anything was promised
/ 03 — The shot list
  1. 01 Dawn orbit, clean face to camera, hills behind
  2. 02 Reveal from behind the ridge onto the roofline
  3. 03 Parallax push along the main road, towers sliding past
  4. 04 Top-down of the plot, phasing and podium readable
  5. 05 Dusk hold as the balcony lights come on
  6. 06 Three vertical cuts, framed on the shoot, not cropped after
/ 04 — Delivered

One graded launch film, a set of stills sized for print and listings, and vertical cuts for the sales team’s feed. Two flying days, one held in reserve for weather. [ Durations, counts and fees set per project ]

Moves
/ 09Scene 0906:36 · SunriseAlt 045 m

Six Moves That Do the Work.

Anyone can put a drone in the air. The difference is knowing which move answers which question, and flying it cleanly enough to cut.

01

Orbit

A circle held at constant height and distance, the building locked in the centre of frame.

/ Answers What does it look like from every side, and what sits behind it.

02

Reveal

Begins with the subject hidden behind a ridge, a roof or a neighbouring block, then rises to disclose it.

/ Answers How do we open the film so nobody looks away.

03

Fly-Through

A controlled pass through a gap: between two towers, across a podium, under a bridge of landscaping.

/ Answers How big is the space between buildings, really.

04

Top-Down

Straight down at ninety degrees, the site read as a plan instead of a picture.

/ Answers Where does the plot end, and how is the phasing laid out.

05

Parallax Push

Low and lateral, with something close to the lens crossing frame while the distance holds steady.

/ Answers How far back does this site actually go.

06

Progress Lock-Off

The same coordinates, the same height, the same framing, flown again every visit.

/ Answers What has actually been built since the last investor update.

/ 11Scene 1111:00 · MiddayOn the ground

How a Shoot Actually Runs.

Most of a good aerial shoot is decided on the ground. By the time we fly, every frame has already been argued about. Timings are agreed per project.

  1. 01

    Brief

    The site, the launch date and who the film has to convince. We come back with questions, a scope and a number.

  2. 02

    Airspace

    We look the site up on the Digital Sky map, read the zone it falls in, and line up whatever permission that zone asks for.

  3. 03

    Flight Plan

    A shot list built around the sun: dawn for clean light on the façade, dusk for the glow the balconies sell on.

  4. 04

    Shoot Day

    Pilot and camera operator on site, flying the list, plus the coverage the building asks for once we can see it.

  5. 05

    Grade & Deliver

    Cut, coloured and delivered by link in the formats your team actually posts. Revisions: [ number to confirm ].
